Subjects needed for law

Law is open to learners from many subject combinations. English and a strong APS matter most.

Updated 26 September 2026

To become an attorney or advocate in South Africa you need an LLB degree. You can study a four-year LLB straight after matric, or first do a BA Law or BCom Law and then complete the LLB.

School subjects you'll usually need

SubjectWhy it matters
EnglishThe most important subject. Law is reading, writing and arguing, so universities usually want a strong English mark.
Mathematics or Mathematical LiteracyMany universities accept either for the LLB. BCom Law usually needs Mathematics.
History, Business Studies or a second languageNot required, but they build the reading, writing and reasoning skills law uses every day.

What makes a law application stronger

LLB places are competitive, so your APS and English mark carry a lot of weight. Debating, public speaking and reading widely all help you prepare.

Always check the latest rules. Each university sets its own subject minimums and APS, and they can change every year. Use this page as a guide, then confirm on the university's prospectus before you choose subjects or apply.

Frequently asked questions

Can I study law with Maths Literacy?

Yes, at many universities. If you want a BCom Law, you'll usually need Mathematics.

Is History required for law?

No. It helps with essay writing and critical thinking, but it isn't a requirement.

How long does it take to become a lawyer?

The LLB takes four years, followed by practical training before you can be admitted as an attorney or advocate.

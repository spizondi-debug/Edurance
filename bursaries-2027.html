Bursaries for 2027: how to find and apply

A practical guide for Grade 11 and 12 learners: where bursaries come from, when to apply and how to give your application the best chance.

Updated 25 September 2026

A bursary pays some or all of your study costs, and unlike a loan you usually don't pay it back. Many bursaries ask you to keep your marks up, and some ask you to work for the sponsor for a set time after you graduate.

Types of bursaries in South Africa

  • Government bursaries. NSFAS is the biggest, for public universities and TVET colleges. The Funza Lushaka bursary supports students training to become teachers.
  • Company bursaries. Large employers in fields such as mining, engineering, finance, accounting and IT fund students in the careers they hire for, often with a work-back agreement.
  • University bursaries and merit awards. Most universities offer their own funding for strong results or financial need. Check the financial aid page of each university you apply to.
  • SETA and sector bursaries. Sector Education and Training Authorities fund study in scarce skills for their industry.
  • Provincial and municipal bursaries. Many provinces and municipalities fund local learners, often in priority fields.

When to apply

Start in Grade 11, and apply in Grade 12. Many company and university bursaries open early in the matric year and close months before results come out, so waiting for your final results usually means missing them. Deadlines change every year, so check each sponsor's page and set reminders.

What you'll usually need

  • A certified copy of your ID
  • Your latest school report (Grade 11 final or Grade 12 June results)
  • Proof of household income, for need-based bursaries
  • A motivational letter
  • Proof that you've applied to, or been accepted at, a university or college

How to write a strong motivational letter

  • Say exactly what you want to study and why, in one or two clear sentences.
  • Connect your goal to the sponsor's field. Explain why their bursary fits.
  • Give real examples: a subject you've improved in, a responsibility at home or school, a project you're proud of.
  • Keep it to one page, check the spelling, and write it yourself.

How to avoid bursary scams

  • Real bursaries never charge an application fee. If someone asks for money, it's a scam.
  • Apply on the sponsor's own website, not through WhatsApp links or "agents".
  • Be careful with requests for bank details or one-time PINs.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a bursary with average marks?

Yes. Many bursaries are need-based, and some look for learners studying scarce skills. Strong marks help, but they aren't the only thing sponsors consider.

Can I have NSFAS and another bursary?

Usually not both for the same costs. If another bursary covers your full costs, NSFAS may not fund you.

Do I have to pay a bursary back?

Usually not, but many company bursaries require you to work for the sponsor after you graduate. Read the contract carefully.

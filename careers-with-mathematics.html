Careers with Mathematics

Mathematics keeps the widest range of careers open to you.

Updated 26 September 2026

Mathematics is required for most science, engineering, health and finance degrees. Many of these careers also need Physical Sciences.

Careers that use Mathematics

CareerOther subjects you'll usually need
EngineerPhysical Sciences and English.
ActuaryA very high Mathematics mark.
Data scientist or software developerEnglish; Physical Sciences sometimes.
Doctor or pharmacistPhysical Sciences and Life Sciences.
Chartered accountant, CA(SA)Accounting helps but isn't always required.
ArchitectPhysical Sciences is often required.

Always check the latest rules. Each university sets its own subject minimums and APS, and they can change every year. Use this page as a guide, then confirm on the university's prospectus before you choose subjects or apply.

Study routes that build on Mathematics

Mathematics supports routes in engineering, computer science, data, actuarial and financial fields, economics, quantitative science and mathematics education. The subject keeps options open, but each programme still sets its own mark, APS and supporting-subject requirements.

Before you choose a course

  1. Identify whether Physical Sciences, Accounting or Information Technology is also required.
  2. Compare the modules and professional recognition of the qualification.
  3. Use your latest marks to create a realistic first choice and at least one alternative route.

Frequently asked questions

Why is Mathematics better than Maths Literacy?

It isn't better for everyone, but it keeps more careers open, including engineering, medicine, finance and IT.

What mark do I need in Maths?

It depends on the course. Engineering, actuarial science and medicine usually need high marks. Check each university's requirements.

Which Maths careers are in demand?

Engineering, IT, data science, finance and Maths teaching are consistently in demand in South Africa.
